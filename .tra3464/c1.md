### The three constraints, and how each is actually enforced

**1. Classified on BOTH endpoints, with the producer's own predicate.** `openTickExitRegion()` now LATCHES the market state at the open instant; `release()` reads the closing state and calls `classifyExitInterval` -- the same function `stampExitPass` uses, over the same `isStockMarketOpen` the decoupled gate refuses on. There is no second RTH test in the process.

Re-deriving the opening state at release instead of latching it would classify a region spanning 16:00 ET from two reads of "now" and no read of its own start, which makes the `boundary` bin **structurally unreachable**. There is a test that fails if the latch is removed.

**2. Excluded regions are COUNTED, NOT DROPPED.** `partitionHolds` requires **both** arms:

- the summed identity `lifetime.samples === samples + boundaryRegions + closedRegions + bootRegionsExcluded`, and
- `engines.every(e => e.rth.partitionHolds)`.

Both, because the summed identity alone is satisfiable by a population in which **no member satisfies it** -- engine A over-counts by 2, engine B under-counts by 2, and the book publishes `true`. An aggregate invariant a mixed population can pass while every member fails is not an invariant. There is a test that drives exactly that pair and asserts the published flag is `false`.

**3. The boot guard publishes what it suppressed.** The first region each engine closes is excluded **ahead of** the RTH classification -- a cold-boot region that happens to sit inside RTH is still excluded -- and published rather than dropped.

## One deviation from the spec'd shape, stated explicitly

**At book level I did NOT spell it `bootRegionMs`.** A book has up to 57 engines and each drops its own cold-boot region, so a scalar `bootRegionMs` there would be an aggregate over N engines wearing an unscoped scalar name -- which is this ticket's own defect, one field down. ## One place the spec contradicts itself -- I picked, and here is why

AC4 says the cold-boot region is "excluded from **both**", i.e. from AC1's RTH block and AC2's lifetime block. The Required-shape block says `partitionHolds` is `lifetime.samples === samples + boundary + closed + (bootRegionExcluded ? 1 : 0)`. **These cannot both hold**: if `lifetime` excluded the boot region, that `+1` term would force `partitionHolds` FALSE on every engine that ever booted.

I implemented the **formula**, so `lifetime` counts every closed region including the boot one. Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. It makes the partition **complete and checkable**, which is the stated purpose of the flag. `lifetime.samples` stays the ground-truth total-since-boot denominator and a reader can verify that every region landed somewhere. Under the other reading the total-since-boot count is not published anywhere and the boot region is visible only as a boolean plus a duration.
2. It makes the demotion a **pure move**: `books.<book>.lifetime.tickExitRegionMs` is byte-for-byte what `books.<book>.tickExitRegionMs` published before this commit. A reader diffing the two builds sees the lifetime numbers stand still and the RTH ones appear -- nothing was silently recomputed under a name that did not change.
3. It is the honest difference from TRA-2269's precedent. The first exit PASS produces no interval because there is no predecessor -- that sample genuinely does not exist. A cold-boot REGION genuinely does exist and has a duration; that is precisely why `bootRegionMs` was specified at all, and why the extra `+1` term is in your formula and not in TRA-2269's.

AC4's prose is still satisfied in the sense that matters: the boot region is excluded from the RTH `samples` **and** from the boundary/closed bins -- the two places a classified region can land -- and it is published, not vanished. If you meant the stricter reading, it is a two-line change and the formula moves with it; tell me which.
